## Two common ways to authorise access

**Provider sign-in** can let you grant an application specified permissions without sharing your password. Read the consent screen and confirm the account and requested access.

**An API key or token** may authorise software to use a provider's service. Treat it as a secret. Its permissions and billing exposure depend on the provider and account.

The actual method, available permissions and costs must be checked for the particular tool and task.

## Define the connection

| Question | What to agree |
| --- | --- |
| What does it need to read? | Specific accounts, records, files and date ranges. |
| What may it change? | Any writes, sends, bookings, publishing or spending, with approval rules. |
| When does it run? | A manual trigger or agreed schedule, with limits and failure handling. |
| How do we check it? | A small test, expected output and someone responsible for review. |
| How do we stop it? | Provider revocation, workflow shutdown and handling of any retained copies. |

## Read-only needs checking

Do not assume a connection is read-only because its first task is reading. Check the permissions actually requested. If the provider cannot narrow access enough, discuss a safer alternative before proceeding.

## Check before relying on it

A successful sign-in alone does not establish that a workflow produces correct results. Test the relevant data and actions, including missing information and failures.
