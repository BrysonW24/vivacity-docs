## How the app uses AI

The Vivacity app helps a business answer its customers. When a customer sends a business an enquiry, a text message, an email or a phone message, the app may ask an AI model to write a reply, sort the message, or suggest what the business should do next.

The AI model is given only what that task needs: the customer's own words, and short details the business has approved about itself (for example the services it offers, its area, its prices and its opening times). It is not given the business's other records, and it does not keep a memory of past conversations.

## Where it goes

The app and its database are hosted in Sydney, Australia. Some services the app relies on are overseas:

- the AI model is provided by OpenAI, in the United States. OpenAI does not use what we send to train its models, and may keep it in a safety log for up to 30 days;
- emails are sent and received through Resend, in the United States;
- text messages and phone calls go through Twilio, in the United States. Phone messages are written out by Twilio and the recording is deleted once the words reach the app;
- when a business connects another app (for example its accounting software), the connection is held by our connection provider, Nango;
- the website is served through Cloudflare, which runs worldwide. Cloudflare's human check (Turnstile) runs on our sign-up, sign-in and enquiry forms; see Cloudflare's Turnstile Privacy Addendum at cloudflare.com/turnstile-privacy-policy.

We choose these providers for their security and reliability.

## Decisions made by the app

A business can let the app act for it within rules the business sets. Within those rules, without a person at the business checking first, the app may:

- reply to a customer who contacted the business first;
- offer the customer a time from the times the business has made available, and book it;
- tell the customer a price from the business's own price list;
- pass a conversation to the business and tell the customer it has done so.

To do this it uses the customer's name, contact details and message, and the business's own details, times and prices. The app does not decide whether a business will take on a customer's work, and does not decide anything about credit, employment or access to services. The business can switch any of these off, and can ask the app to wait for its approval before acting.

If a reply or a booking made by the app affects you, contact the business directly.
